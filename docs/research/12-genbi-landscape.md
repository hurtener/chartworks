# Research Brief 12 — GenBI Landscape: Teramot, Semantic Layers, Text-to-SQL Evals, Data Profiling

> Status: draft · 2026-07-06 · sources: web (URLs cited inline)

This is the one brief in the series built entirely from **public web sources** — no
predecessor code was read (per CLAUDE.md's `_ref/` rule; the predecessors are named only
as "the client predecessor" and "the generalistic predecessor" where relevant, and neither
is discussed by name in this brief since it is out of scope). It exists to give the RFC
(CLAUDE.md §1, D-013, D-014) an evidence base for the "beat Teramot-class GenBI" target and
for how the topic-pack semantic layer, the eval harness, and the profiling stage should be
scoped against the wider market.

---

## Summary

- **Teramot** (teramot.com) is an early-stage, thinly-documented GenBI/data-infrastructure
  product: it auto-connects to warehouses/CRMs/ERPs/files, runs a "patented multi-agent"
  pipeline across **Bronze → Silver → Gold** medallion layers to produce auditable,
  reviewable SQL and "Gold Tables," then exposes those Gold Tables to any MCP-speaking AI
  assistant (Claude, ChatGPT, Cursor, etc.) for querying — SOC 2 certified, plan-gated by
  Gold Table count / storage / processing volume. [teramot.com/teramot-dev, g2.com/products/teramot]
- Public sources **do not confirm** the specific "calling agent generates the SQL, platform
  only validates" split the kickoff doc attributes to Teramot; what is confirmed is the
  adjacent, better-evidenced pattern — Teramot's **own** agents generate the pipeline SQL
  (bronze/silver/gold transforms), and it is the **query-time** MCP surface where an
  external assistant (Claude/ChatGPT) is the one issuing natural-language asks against the
  already-built Gold Table. Whether the query-time SQL is generated by Teramot's MCP tool
  or by the calling model itself is not stated on any page this brief could reach — flagged
  as an open question the RFC/D-014 should not assume settled. [teramot.com/teramot-dev]
- Pricing is a **three-plan, usage-metered** model (Gold Table count, lakehouse storage,
  monthly processing volume, add-on Gold Tables) — no public dollar figures found.
  [teramot.com/pricing search snippet]
- The semantic-layer landscape (**Cube**, **dbt MetricFlow**, **Malloy/LookML**) converges
  on four primitives a mature layer must own: **metrics/measures, dimensions, join paths,
  and access policy** — all defined once, compiled to SQL centrally, never left to a
  caller's guess. This is exactly the shape D-013's "topic pack" should be graded against.
  [cube.dev, docs.getdbt.com, docs.malloydata.dev]
- Public text-to-SQL benchmarks (**BIRD**, **Spider 2.0**) show a widening gap between
  toy-schema and enterprise-schema difficulty: BIRD's best public scores sit near
  80-92% (with contested annotation quality), while Spider 2.0's enterprise-scale,
  multi-dialect workflows collapse leading models to ~21% success. [bird-bench.github.io,
  arxiv.org/pdf/2411.07763] The gap is the strongest available argument for grounding
  generation in a semantic layer (our approach) rather than raw schema (theirs).
- Data-quality/profiling tooling (**Great Expectations**-class) organizes checks around six
  standard dimensions — completeness, uniqueness, validity, consistency, integrity,
  timeliness — a good V1 checklist for the engineering stage's profiling step.
  [docs.greatexpectations.io]

## Teramot

### What the public surface says

Teramot's own marketing surface (`teramot.com`) is a client-rendered site with very thin
server-rendered content; direct fetches of `/`, `/docs`, `/mission`, and
`/blog/data-engineering-tools` returned only page-shell headers ("Hacé que la IA entienda
tu empresa" / "Make AI understand your business"; "Documentación · Teramot"; "Nuestra
Misión"; a 404 on the blog slug tried) — the substantive copy is loaded client-side and
was only recoverable through search-engine cached snippets, not a direct fetch. This
brief's Teramot claims are therefore sourced from **search-result snippets of Teramot's
own pages** (`teramot.com/teramot-dev`, `teramot.com/docs`, `teramot.com/pricing`) and from
a third-party listing (G2). Where a claim could only be confirmed via a snippet and not a
full fetch, it is marked *(snippet-only)*.

**Positioning.** Tagline: "Hacé que la IA entienda tu empresa" (Make AI understand your
business) [teramot.com]. Sub-positioning: "Teramot — Powering the AI Agent Era"
[teramot.com/teramot-dev]. It targets teams who want AI assistants to act on real,
governed enterprise data rather than raw warehouse access.

**Core mechanism — the medallion pipeline *(snippet-only)*.** Teramot connects directly to
existing databases, warehouses, ERPs, CRMs, and files, then runs what it calls a
"patented multi-agent system" (reported as "+20 agents") across three layers:

- **Bronze** — exact, as-ingested replica of source data, no transformation.
- **Silver** — cleaning and standardization.
- **Gold** — business logic: aggregations, calculations, KPIs — the business-ready,
  queryable output, called a **Gold Table**.

"Teramot's agents analyze your schema, detect data quality issues, propose
transformations, and generate transparent, auditable SQL that you can review before
deploying." [g2.com/products/teramot, teramot.com/teramot-dev search snippets] The pitch
to the end user is explicitly no-code / no-SQL: "Tell your AI what you want to achieve, in
the language you use to think about your business. No SQL or technical specs required."
*(snippet-only, teramot.com)*

**Ontology / schema-understanding story.** "Teramot automatically reads your schema and
catalogs your tables and relationships. Once connected to your data sources, your AI uses
Teramot to understand how your business works by learning what tables exist, how they
relate, and what they mean" *(snippet-only, teramot.com/mission and teramot.com/docs
search results)*. This is a lighter, more automated claim than a hand-authored semantic
layer (contrast with Cube/MetricFlow below, which are explicitly author-defined) — it
reads as **inferred** ontology (agent-derived from schema + data samples) rather than
**declared** ontology (an analyst writing metric/dimension/join definitions). No public
page describes the inference method, confidence handling, or a human review/approval step
beyond "review the SQL before deploying."

**MCP / calling-agent story.** Deployed Gold Tables are exposed via **MCP (Model Context
Protocol)** and are described as queryable "from Claude, ChatGPT, or any AI assistant"
[teramot.com/teramot-dev, teramot.com/mission search snippets], and separately listed as
integrating with "Claude, Claude Code, ChatGPT, Codex, Cursor, Gemini, and any AI that
supports the standard" [search snippet, teramot.com/mission]. **What this brief could not
verify**: the kickoff framing ("their calling agent generates the SQL and the platform
validates") describes a query-time division of labor — does the external AI assistant
(the MCP client) generate the SQL against the Gold Table schema itself, with Teramot's MCP
server only validating/executing it, or does Teramot's MCP tool generate the SQL
server-side from the assistant's natural-language ask? No page reachable by this brief
states this explicitly. The closest public analogy found is the general MCP-ecosystem
pattern of a read-only SQL-executing MCP tool that AST-parses and allowlists whatever SQL
the calling model supplies before executing it [glama.ai/mcp/servers/tahasiddiquii/mcp-sql-guard,
general MCP text-to-SQL guardrail pattern — not Teramot-specific] — which is *consistent*
with, but does not *confirm*, the kickoff's characterization. **This is exactly the shape
D-014's "BYO-agent" mode already generalizes**: regardless of which precise wiring Teramot
uses, Chartworks' committed design (Chartworks hands a calling agent the topic
pack/context slice; the agent generates SQL; Chartworks validates and executes under the
same gates internal generation uses) covers the Teramot pattern as one instance and is not
dependent on resolving this ambiguity.

**Data sources.** PostgreSQL, MySQL, Microsoft SQL Server, Oracle, Google BigQuery,
Snowflake, Amazon Redshift, Salesforce, SAP HANA, CSV files, Google Sheets, Excel
[g2.com/products/teramot search snippet]. Downstream integration is aimed at existing BI
tools — Power BI, Looker, Tableau, Metabase — positioning Gold Tables as a governed
substrate under a company's current BI stack rather than a BI replacement.

**Trust/compliance claim.** "SOC 2 certified" *(snippet-only, g2.com)* — not independently
verified against a certificate registry by this brief; treat as a **marketing claim** until
corroborated.

**Pricing *(marketing claim, no dollar figures found)*.** "Simple, predictable monthly
pricing" across three tiers — individual analysts/small projects, analytics
teams/departments, large teams/high-volume operations — metered on: number of active Gold
Tables, total storage in the managed lakehouse (raw + transformed), and monthly processing
volume (ETL runs + transformations + query execution); additional Gold Tables sold as
add-ons on every tier [teramot.com/pricing search snippet]. No public number for any tier
was found; the pricing page itself renders client-side and returned only its title on
direct fetch.

**Funding / company signal.** No YC listing, funding announcement, or press coverage was
found under the name "Teramot" — consistent with a small or early-stage company whose
public footprint is close to its own marketing site plus one G2 listing (2 reviews). Do
not treat Teramot as a scale-validated benchmark, only a directional one.

## Semantic-layer landscape

**Cube.** A semantic layer sitting between the warehouse and every consumer (BI tool,
embedded app, spreadsheet, AI agent): "metrics, dimensions, join paths, and access rules"
are defined once, as code, in "cubes" representing business entities (customers, orders,
line items), each carrying measures, dimensions, joins, pre-aggregations, and access
policies; a request like "revenue by region for the last four quarters" compiles through
the defined join paths and metric logic rather than a caller's guess. Row/column access
policies are code (Python/JS) and can range from simple row-level rules to fully
tenant-aware models spanning multiple data sources; the strongest implementations enforce
access **before** SQL generation. [cube.dev/articles/what-is-a-semantic-layer,
docs.cube.dev/docs/product/data-modeling/reference/cube,
cube.dev/articles/semantic-layer-for-ai-agents-2026]

→ **Chartworks landing:** the topic pack should own the same four primitives —
measures/metrics, dimensions, join paths, access — and, per P1, must enforce access
**inside** the query path exactly as Cube's "strongest implementations" do; a topic pack
without a first-class access-policy field, evaluated at query-compile time rather than
after, would be a regression against the state of the art, not merely against the RFC.

**dbt MetricFlow (dbt Semantic Layer).** MetricFlow defines and manages metric logic
through **semantic models** (each a 1:1 wrapper on a dbt model, carrying a default time
dimension for aggregation), **metrics** (functions producing quantitative indicators over
the semantic-model graph), and **dimensions** (the group-by axes — region, country, job
title). Joins are built from entity-key types the semantic models declare; MetricFlow
"chooses the appropriate join type and avoids fan-out or chasm joins," and lets a single
metric span multiple semantic models. [docs.getdbt.com/docs/build/about-metricflow,
docs.getdbt.com/docs/build/semantic-models, docs.getdbt.com/docs/build/join-logic]

→ **Chartworks landing:** MetricFlow's fan-out/chasm-trap avoidance is a concrete,
non-obvious correctness property (a naive join-graph router silently overcounts on
one-to-many joins) — the topic pack's join-graph resolution should explicitly test against
fan-out/chasm scenarios, not just "joins resolve," mirroring the adversarial-test
discipline CLAUDE.md §11 already mandates for access paths.

**Malloy / LookML (brief).** LookML defines dimensions (attributes to group by) and
measures (aggregatable quantities) plus their relationships in Looker's own modeling
language, trading some query flexibility for safety. Malloy is pitched as the flexibility
upgrade — "the safety of a semantic data model with the full flexibility of a relational
query language" — treating the semantic model as a real, composable language rather than a
YAML config. [Devoteam LookML-vs-dbt comparison; docs.malloydata.dev/documentation/user_guides/quickstart_modeling;
docs.malloydata.dev/blog/2023-11-06-beyond-yaml]

→ **Chartworks landing:** the safety-vs-flexibility tension Malloy names is the same one
the topic pack faces when a BYO-agent (D-014) asks a question the pack doesn't cleanly
cover — worth an explicit RFC decision on whether the pack is a closed vocabulary (LookML-
style, safer, more restrictive) or an extensible query surface (Malloy-style, more
flexible, harder to validate) for the SQL-safety gate (P1) to hold against.

## Text-to-SQL evaluation landscape

**BIRD.** ~12,751 question-SQL pairs across 95 databases (33.4 GB), 37+ professional
domains, explicitly designed to include "dirty data" and complex, real-world schemas
rather than toy databases. [bird-bench.github.io, arxiv.org/pdf/2308.15363] Reported
leaderboard state as of this brief: a leading system pairing "AskData + GPT-4o" near
81.95% on the test set against a ~92.96% human ceiling in one report; a separate leaderboard
snapshot (Bird-SQL dev) shows Gemini 2.0 Flash-Lite leading at 0.574 across 7 evaluated
models — the spread between sources shows leaderboard volatility and metric-definition
sensitivity. [llm-stats.com/benchmarks/bird-sql-(dev), explainx.ai/blog/google-gemini-sql2-text-to-sql-bird-benchmark-2026]
A VLDB 2026 paper and a companion GitHub repo document **pervasive annotation errors** in
BIRD and other text-to-SQL benchmarks/leaderboards, with corrected-annotation performance
swings of −2% to +19% absolute and rank changes of −2 to +3 positions.
[vldb.org/cidrdb/papers/2026/p5-jin.pdf, github.com/uiuc-kang-lab/text_to_sql_benchmarks]

**Spider 2.0.** 632 real-world enterprise text-to-SQL workflow problems, databases sourced
from actual production use cases, often 1,000-3,000+ columns, spanning multiple SQL
dialects (BigQuery, Snowflake, etc.) and multi-step operations (transformation +
analytics, not single-shot lookup). [arxiv.org/pdf/2411.07763, spider2-sql.github.io] Even
the most advanced models struggle badly: one report puts o1-preview-class performance at
21.3% success, contrasted against original Spider's ~86% — the "Spider (86%) → Spider 2.0
(6-21%)" collapse is repeatedly cited as evidence that toy-schema benchmarks vastly
overstate real capability. [emergentmind.com/topics/spider-2-0-benchmark]

→ **What a private, semantic-layer-grounded eval should borrow.** Both public benchmarks
are fundamentally **schema-only**: the model sees raw DDL/column names and must infer
business meaning and correct joins cold, which is exactly the difficulty Spider 2.0
exposes at enterprise scale. Chartworks' `eval/` harness (CLAUDE.md §3) is **model-grounded**
— the topic pack already hands the model curated measures/dimensions/joins/access, i.e.
the harder half of Spider 2.0's failure mode (schema/join discovery) is supposed to be
solved by the semantic layer *before* generation, not left to the model. This means:
(a) Chartworks' harness should still borrow BIRD/Spider's **discipline** — real, messy,
production-shaped schemas; multi-dialect coverage; execution-result comparison, not just
SQL-text similarity — but (b) it should score topic-pack-grounded generation as its own
category, since a raw head-to-head against BIRD/Spider numbers would be comparing
different problems (grounded vs. ungrounded) and could mislead the RFC into over- or
under-confidence. (c) The annotation-error lesson (BIRD/Spider both suffer from mislabeled
gold SQL) argues for the eval harness treating its own golden fixtures as a maintained,
versioned asset subject to the same review rigor CLAUDE.md §11 requires of golden tests
generally — not a one-time authoring exercise.

## Data-profiling / quality landscape (brief)

**Great Expectations (GX)** is the reference open-source framework: checks ("Expectations")
are typed, parameterized assertions (e.g. `expect_column_values_to_be_between`,
`expect_column_values_to_be_unique`) organized around standard data-quality dimensions —
**completeness, uniqueness, validity, consistency, integrity, timeliness** — validating
value ranges, relationships/dependencies between columns, structural conformance, and
record-count bounds, with metadata (unique IDs) for tracking. It integrates with
Airflow/Snowflake/Databricks/cloud storage as a pluggable validation stage.
[docs.greatexpectations.io/docs/reference/learn/data_quality_use_cases/dq_use_cases_lp,
docs.greatexpectations.io/docs/reference/learn/data_quality_use_cases/integrity,
kdnuggets.com/2023/03/data-quality-dimensions-assuring-data-quality-great-expectations]

→ **Chartworks landing:** the V1 profiling stage (D-013: "profiling/quality checks, schema
inference") should ship at minimum one check per GX-standard dimension — completeness
(null/missing rates), uniqueness (duplicate/key-candidate detection), validity (type/range/
format conformance), consistency (cross-column and cross-table invariants), integrity
(referential/FK-shape checks against the inferred join graph), and timeliness (freshness/
staleness of ingested data) — rather than an ad hoc subset. This gives the engineering
stage a recognizable, industry-standard vocabulary instead of a bespoke one (also relevant
to P6 domain-vocabulary discipline: name these checks in domain terms a user recognizes,
not GX's internal terminology verbatim).

Other reference points worth a name-check but not deep coverage here: **Soda Core** and
**dbt tests / `dbt-expectations`** cover largely the same six-dimension space with
different DSLs; **Monte Carlo**/​**Bigeye**-class commercial "data observability" tools add
anomaly-detection-over-time on top of the same static-check foundation — a plausible V2
direction (drift/anomaly detection) once V1's static checks are solid, not a V1
requirement.

## Open questions (for the RFC / phase planners)

1. **Teramot's query-time SQL-generation split is unconfirmed.** Before D-014's BYO-agent
   mode is described in any external-facing doc as "matching Teramot's pattern," find a
   primary source (API reference, demo video, or a Teramot engineering blog post) that
   states whether the MCP client (the calling assistant) or Teramot's own MCP-side tool
   generates the query-time SQL. Absent that, describe Chartworks' BYO-agent mode on its
   own merits (already done in D-014's rationale), not as a confirmed parity claim.
2. **Ontology inference vs. declaration.** Teramot claims automatic schema/relationship
   cataloging with no visible review-and-correct workflow beyond "review the SQL." The RFC
   should decide explicitly whether Chartworks' topic-pack authoring is (a) fully
   human-authored, (b) agent-assisted-with-mandatory-human-approval, or (c) agent-inferred
   with optional review — and should not default to (c) by omission just because a
   competitor markets it.
2b. Relatedly: should the profiling/schema-inference stage (D-013) produce a
   **confidence score** per inferred relationship/column-role, surfaced to the human
   reviewer, given GX-class tools and Teramot both lack a documented confidence UX?
3. **Fan-out/chasm-join correctness tests** are a MetricFlow-documented failure mode not
   yet named in any Chartworks phase plan; the join-graph resolution component (wherever it
   lands — `internal/semantics`, per CLAUDE.md §3) should carry an explicit adversarial test
   for this, tracked at that phase's authoring time (§16 workflow).
4. **Closed vs. extensible topic-pack vocabulary** (the Malloy/LookML tension) is
   unresolved — pin it in the RFC alongside the P1 SQL-safety mechanism, since an
   extensible pack raises the bar on injection/allowlisting validation.
5. **Eval harness category separation** (grounded vs. ungrounded generation) should be an
   explicit `eval/` design decision, not an implicit assumption — a follow-up brief or RFC
   appendix should define the harness's scoring categories before phase implementation
   starts building fixtures.
6. **Pricing/positioning** intelligence on Teramot is too thin to inform go-to-market;
   this is flagged for whoever owns Chartworks' business-side decisions, not an engineering
   blocker.
